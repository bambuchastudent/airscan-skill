**Scope:** carrier-local dated schedules, not airport-wide topology; a named product/API existing does NOT establish VLC schedule coverage.

Evidence labels: **OFFICIAL** airline/operator published page; **CODE** inspected upstream source; **PARTNER** controlled/contracted access; **UNVERIFIED** no current VLC or actual HTTP response verified; **RESTRICTED/UNCLEAR** terms or permission gate means do not automate until clarified; **ESTIMATE** arithmetic only, not a measurement. A source can bear multiple labels.

Unknown for every provider below unless explicitly evidenced: exact active VLC route set, guaranteed dated-availability horizon for VLC, quota/real-world speed, rights to retain historical results in AirScan, and true saleable seat inventory.

## P1 evidence conclusions

1. At least two meaningful multi-airline strategies exist (OAG published schedules; Aviation Edge predicted airport-day schedules), but neither is currently proven to give a permitted **VLC** 180-day feed with appropriate local storage rights and affordable cost.
2. **Only the licensed/provider-access route can be assessed as an unattended production option now**. Flywizz and Ryanair OSS are excellent code-level capability references, not approved scraping integrations.
3. Vueling officially exposes flight-calendar capability through *some* service providers. The partner matrix is per provider and NOT a blanket public feature claim.
4. The user-value target is `VLC known topology + positive confirmed date evidence + explicit uncovered`; predicted schedules must remain distinct. No count/coverage percentage is reported without a validated Aena snapshot.
5. P1 does not fulfill P2 live probes, P3 measured costs, or P4 final choice. Research gate stays **IN PROGRESS**.
